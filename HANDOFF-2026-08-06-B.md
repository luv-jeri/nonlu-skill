## What shipped

### pixel — video input, ground-truth tested
`scripts/motion.py` — `probe · events · measure · compare · keyframes · camera · selftest`.

Fills the Micro-interactions table and Interaction Audit that a still image
never could. Verified against animations with known values:

| Truth | Measured |
|---|---|
| 400ms ease-out | 400ms ease-out (exact) |
| 700ms linear | 720ms linear |
| 250ms ease-in-out | 240ms ease-in-out |

**Three defects the ground-truth test caught**, all in the dangerous direction:
1. Coarse event bounds truncate any easing with a slow tail — a tail-less
   ease-out is indistinguishable from linear.
2. That refinement must UNION with the coarse bounds, never intersect: a
   translation's diff signal saturates and a 700ms slide collapsed to 200ms.
3. **"Nothing moved" on a 10s camera flythrough.** The noise floor came from the
   MEDIAN frame-to-frame change, which only holds when most frames are still.
   UI clip: 76% still frames, peak/floor ratio 2.7e6. Cinematic: **0% still,
   ratio 3.6.** With no quiet baseline the median IS the motion.

   *General lesson worth carrying anywhere:* a threshold derived from the data it
   thresholds inverts silently when the data's shape changes. No error — just a
   confident wrong answer.

## The headline finding

Building one cinematic three ways:

| | Weight | FPS | Dropped | Looks real |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A · scrub real frames | 5,255 kb | 59.3 | 1% | yes |
| B · procedural | **749 kb** | 45.2 | 24.9% | **no** |
| C · hybrid | 5,925 kb | **58.8** | **2.1%** | **yes** |

Hand-written GLSL cannot produce photorealistic volumetric water at this budget.
B is 8x lighter and still loses on the thing that was asked for.

**The pattern that ships**: real footage bound as a GPU texture with a shader
over it. Several Awwwards heroes are tagged "video + shader + filter". The
footage supplies realism; the shader supplies refraction displacement,
chromatic aberration, wavelength-dependent depth absorption, a filmic curve and
animated grain. C beat B on fps, dropped frames AND blocking — the GPU decodes
video far more cheaply than it evaluates procedural shaders.

**"Pixel by pixel from video to code" holds for UI and not for cinematics.** A
chat interface rebuilt from a screenshot the same night hit **2.0% mean pixel
difference**. This did not come close.

## Perf lessons — do not relearn these

- Particle motion belongs in the vertex shader. 1,200 points in a JS loop with a
  per-frame buffer upload: 33.1fps / 47.7% dropped. On the GPU: 59.9 / 0%.
- Clamp `gl_PointSize`. Uncapped it peaked ~180px; bubbles read as bokeh snow.
- Bake procedural patterns to a seamless tile at startup, animate UVs. Live
  per-fragment Worley: 26.4fps / 97.5% dropped. Baked: 45.5fps.
- Combine two scrolling copies of a line tile with `max()`, not multiply — a
  product of thin bright lines on black is near-zero except at intersections.
- `ACESFilmicToneMapping` + exposure is the cheapest cinematic win available.
- An average-FPS number that starts sampling at load conflates startup cost with
  sustained smoothness. Read dropped-frame % alongside it.
